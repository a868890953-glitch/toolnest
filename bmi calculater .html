About Our BMI Calculator

Understanding basic health measurements can be useful when learning more about your body and general wellness. The calculator supports both metric and imperial measurements, making it convenient for users in different countries.

BMI stands for Body Mass Index. It is a numerical screening measurement calculated using a person's height and weight. For adults, BMI is commonly calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by height in meters squared. The resulting number can then be compared with commonly used adult BMI categories. These categories are generally described as underweight, healthy weight, overweight and obesity. BMI can therefore be useful as one starting point for understanding weight status, but it should not be considered a complete measurement of a person's health.

Our BMI calculator has been created with simplicity in mind. You do not need a complicated application or special software to use it. One of the useful features of this tool is the ability to switch between metric and imperial units. Metric users can enter height in centimeters and weight in kilograms, while imperial users can enter height in feet and inches and weight in pounds. This makes the calculator flexible for a wide range of users. The responsive design also means that the calculator can be used comfortably on smartphones, tablets, laptops and desktop computers.

It is important to understand what a BMI result can and cannot tell you. BMI does not directly measure body fat, muscle mass, fitness, nutrition, physical activity, genetics or overall health. Two people with the same BMI may have very different body compositions and health profiles. Factors such as age, muscle development, medical history, lifestyle and other measurements can provide important additional context. Therefore, a BMI result should be viewed as a screening indicator rather than a diagnosis.

BMI interpretation is also different for children and teenagers. Adult BMI categories should not automatically be applied to younger people. For children and adolescents, healthcare professionals generally consider BMI in relation to age and sex using growth charts or BMI percentiles. If you are under 18 and want to understand what your BMI means, it is better to discuss the result with a parent, guardian, pediatrician, doctor or another qualified healthcare professional rather than comparing your number with adult categories.

For adults, BMI can be useful when considered together with other health information. A healthcare professional may look at blood pressure, blood tests, waist measurements, medical history, physical activity, diet and other factors when evaluating overall health. This is why a single calculator result should not be used to make major health decisions. Instead, use this tool as an educational starting point and seek professional guidance when you have concerns about your health.

Frequently Asked Questions About BMI

What does BMI mean?

BMI means Body Mass Index. It is a screening measurement based on height and weight that is commonly used to classify weight status in adults.

How is BMI calculated?

For adults using metric measurements, BMI is calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by height in meters squared.

Is BMI an accurate measure of health?

BMI can be useful as a screening measure, but it does not provide a complete picture of health or body composition. It should be considered alongside other relevant factors.

Can teenagers use adult BMI categories?

No. Children and teenagers are generally assessed using age- and sex-specific BMI percentiles rather than the standard adult categories.
